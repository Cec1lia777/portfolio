Nostal-Navi

A memory-driven life-path navigator for Gen Z

Bronze · Spark Design Award 2024.07 — 2025.02 Role: UX, research, front-end, 3D Figma · Cinema 4D · TypeScript · TouchDesigner

Nostal-Navi is an interactive website that helps young people aged 16–25 navigate life-path development by reflecting on past experiences. Grounded in narrative psychology, it turns nostalgia from passive escape into a growth tool: an AI-personalised chat agent, a 3D “room” that grows with the user, a community positioning map, and long-term goal roadmaps. I ran a 120-response user survey, built the service blueprint, modelled the room in Cinema 4D, and programmed the interface in TypeScript.

Chefeon QuickBite

A semi-automatic dorm cooking system — internship project

Internship · Product Strategy 2025.08 Supervisor: Dr. Steve Hua (Caltech PhD) User research · service system · product design

At Chefeon (Shanghai Changshan Intelligent Technology), I studied the user experience and automation mechanics of a cooking robot and designed a dorm cooking system for overseas universities. I interviewed international students from the UK, US and Singapore, mapped their pain points — expensive and inauthentic Chinese food, no time to cook — and translated them into a numbered ingredient-pack system, a self-cleaning machine workflow, and a business plan for campus deployment.

Advanced Ambulance Avoidance System

45-degree angled parking + a connected give-way reminder — CTB research project

Third Place · CTB Global Finals at Harvard 2023.09 — 2024.06 Role: team lead, prototyping, media campaign STM32 · Bluetooth · 433MHz · App

Ambulance delays cost lives: over 30% of patients have treatments delayed by traffic jams. I led a team researching the problem — 300+ survey responses, interviews with traffic police, nurses and drivers — and built a two-part answer: a hardware give-way system (STM32 main board, Bluetooth and 433MHz modules, in-car LCD guidance) and a new-media campaign teaching 45-degree angled parking. The guidance video raised test accuracy from 0% to 90%; the campaign reached 6,700+ views across WeChat, Bilibili and TikTok. Our paper was published on Young Scholars Academic.

LumiReed

An eye-tracking assistive reading tool for visually-impaired children

Self-initiated · with Bethel China 2023.10 — 2024.06 Role: product design, user research, prototyping Product design · Eye-tracking · TypeScript

While volunteering as an English teacher for a visually-impaired child, I noticed him straining to locate words on the page. LumiReed began there. I ran two controlled experiments with VI-simulating glasses and a webcam eye-tracker, interviewed four children aged 6–11 about the tools they use, and analysed the weaknesses of existing magnifiers. The result is a screen-based reading device that follows the child’s gaze to zoom, highlight and recolour text — validated in low-fidelity tests and a Tobii eye-tracking session.

To Be or Not?

A time-based interactive installation on end-of-life decisions

Northwestern University · Bioethics (A) 2025.06 — 2025.09 Speculative design · kinetic sculpture · projection mapping 180 × 205 cm · Mixed media · Arduino · TouchDesigner

Born from my Northwestern bioethics course, this installation asks: at the end of life, who decides — and how? Visitors face the case of patient C-1137 and steer a kinetic “spine” sculpture through a projection-mapped hospital corridor. Choosing “to be” speeds up the machinery of treatment — faster heartbeat, ventilator, pain; choosing “not to be” slows everything toward silence. The mechanics run on Arduino servos and DFRobot sensors, with TouchDesigner driving projection and sound.

06

BeanFam

An eco-sustainable mung bean factory — from soil remediation to biomaterial

Supervisor: Mr. Kun Zhao 2025.04 — 2025.06 Sustainability research · biomaterial experiments Bio-based material

Roughly 14–17% of global croplands exceed safe copper levels. BeanFam explores the mung bean as a remediation engine: inoculated with Rhizobium, the plants cut copper concentration in contaminated soil by 29% (vs 8% in the control). The harvest becomes food and juice; the waste becomes bioplastic. I documented full growth cycles, ran sucrose and vitamin-C experiments, and prototyped gelatin–glycerol sheets from sprout and root waste.
